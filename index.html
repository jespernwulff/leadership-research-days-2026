Pillar 2 · the precision ladder

Three rungs, each one a harder test

  • 1Direction — "a leader with more followers gets more done" → test H₀: β = 0.Where almost every paper stops.
  • 2Model-implied regime — "at a diminishing rate" → reject both β ≤ 0 and β ≥ 1 (two one-sided tests).The boundaries 0 and 1 come from the log-link model you already run: an elasticity below 1 is diminishing returns, above 1 increasing returns. Finite attention predicts β < 1; a vision that reaches every follower at once predicts β = 1; followers learning from each other predicts β > 1. "More is better" is silent on the shape; the regime test is not.
  • 3Theory-implied interval — a formal model pins β to a value or a narrow range; test equivalence to it.Leadership version: Simonton's model fixes the optimum of perceived leadership at 1.2 SD above the group's mean IQ; the test is equivalence to that value (Antonakis, House & Simonton 2017).

Pillar 3 · from statistical to practical significance

Four moves from "p < .05" to "this matters"

1
p < .05 only says "not exactly zero"
With enough data it says that about every effect.
2
Declare the smallest effect that matters, before the data
From the field's benchmarks, from what the programme costs, or from the model's own boundaries.
3
Test against it, with α that shrinks as n grows
Two one-sided tests against the band. A fixed 5% is far too lax at 2,000 per group (Wulff & Taylor 2024).
4
Report in the units decisions are made in
A charismatic speech: +17% output (Antonakis et al. 2022). One SD of manager talent: +10% productivity (Fenizia 2022).

Yesterday, through three lenses

Three questions to ask of every talk from day one

Pillar 1 · model
Which assumption was treated as exact?
No confounder, parallel trends, exclusion. What is the breakdown value, and would the author bet on it?
Pillar 2 · hypothesis
Was that a direction, or a regime?
Could a rival mechanism have "predicted" the same sign? What shape or range would the theory have to defend?
Pillar 3 · test
"No effect" — compared with what?
Was a smallest effect of interest declared? Was the null result an equivalence result, or just an underpowered one?
